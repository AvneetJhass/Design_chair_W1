Step 1 Empathise Know Your User

Before designing anything, you need to deeply understand the person you are designing for. Empathy means seeing the world through their eyes — their needs, frustrations, and goals. Identify who your user is and their daily context. Discover what problems they already face with existing chairs. Understand both their physical and emotional needs.

💡 Tip Think about someone specific — a grandparent, a student with a disability, a gamer, or a young child. The more specific your user, the stronger your design will be.
📚 Reference Talk to a real person if you can — ask them how they feel sitting for long periods.

Step 2 Research Study Real Chairs

Good designers never start from zero — they learn from what already exists. Research helps you identify what works, what fails, and where the real opportunity for improvement lies. Find 2 real chairs used by someone similar to your user. Analyse what is good and bad about each chair, and identify specific improvements.

💡 Tip Search "[your user] + chair" on Google Images or YouTube. Try: ergonomic chair, classroom chair, wheelchair, gaming chair, rocking chair.
📚 Reference Pinterest, Google Images, IKEA.com, HermanMiller.com, Kogan.com

Step 3 Define State the Problem

Now that you know your user and have done your research, write a clear problem statement. This single focused sentence will guide every design decision you make. Use your research and empathy findings to pinpoint the core problem.

💡 Tip Specific beats vague! "Uncomfortable" is too vague. Try: "No lumbar support for a student sitting 6+ hours daily, causing back pain and poor posture."

Step 4 Design Influences What Will Shape Your Design?

Every great design is guided by clear principles and constraints. Before you sketch anything, choose the values that will shape your decisions. These become the lens through which you evaluate every design choice.

💡 Tip Ergonomics = designing so the product fits the human body naturally. Inclusivity = designing so anyone can use it, regardless of age or ability.

💡 Tip Think of your specification as a promise to your user. Every decision you make during prototyping should come back to this list.

Step 5 Ideate Sketch Your Design

Now the creative work begins! Generate ideas before committing to one. At this stage, quantity beats quality — the goal is to explore as many possibilities as you can before narrowing down. Sketch a quick first draft, then refine your best idea. Label all key parts clearly.

💡 Tip Draw at least 2 ideas before choosing your favourite. Add labels, arrows, and notes — a sketch with annotations is far more useful than a perfect drawing.
